01 / Research

Thesis: one photo instead of a diameter tape

Multitask learning for tree instance segmentation and diameter estimation from RGB-D imagery

Forest inventories turn standing trees into numbers that carbon and policy decisions depend on, and in Indonesia they are still mostly measured by hand. I extended MaskDINO with a FocalNet-L backbone to accept a fourth, depth channel, and added a head that estimates diameter at breast height (DBH) from backbone features at the 1.3 m row. One network returns species-aware instance masks and diameters in a single pass.

62.29%test AP50, species-aware segmentation
7.62 cmDBH RMSE, end-to-end on own detections
+12.92AP50 points from adding depth (controlled)

Read with care. All data is synthetic (Unreal Engine 5), and diameter is returned for about 27% of eligible trees; most of the shortfall is trees the model never detects. The case study states these limits next to the results.

04 / Approach

How I work, shown by the thesis

Isolate the variable

Comparing RGB with RGB-D usually mixes two effects: depth, and whether the model starts from pretrained weights. A four-channel stem cannot inherit them. With both arms pretrained, depth looks worth under one AP50 point; with both trained from scratch it is worth 12.92.

Paper, section 3.2 / results

Evaluate what would be deployed

Diameter is scored on the model's own detections and masks, not on ground truth. Of 3,034 trees the system failed on, 3,012 were simply never detected, only 22 were misnamed. That says where to work next.

Paper, section 3.5 / distance breakdown

Report what did not work

Raising the diameter loss weight cost roughly 5.9% in segmentation loss and gave no measurable diameter gain, so the final model keeps a constant weight. A geometric diameter baseline is reported alongside the learned head, which beats it by about a factor of two.

Paper, sections 3.4 and 3.5.1 / limitations
